Air Quest was an educational civic-tech experiment developed through the UC Berkeley Social Apps Lab in partnership with UC Santa Barbara and UC Santa Cruz researchers.

## 01 // The Sensory & Physical Brief
California's Central Valley suffers from some of the nation's worst air quality and highest pediatric asthma rates. The objective was to translate dense atmospheric chemistry and respiratory telemetry into an engaging, accessible six-level iPad game for elementary school children and pediatric patients.

## 02 // Bespoke Hardware & Algorithmic Stack
* **Unity3D Multi-Level Engine:** Built a custom multi-level 2D game engine in Unity3D for early iOS iPads, balancing interactive physics with low memory footprints.
* **Asset Pipeline Conversion:** Collaborated with academic biologists and environmental scientists who were not trained game artists—transforming their scientific diagrams and vector illustrations into rigged 2D sprite sheets, particle effects, and interactive mini-game loops.

## 03 // Live Deployment & What Broke
Navigating academic ambiguity was the primary challenge: the research leadership had expansive, theoretical visions but lacked concrete interactive design specifications. I had to rapidly bridge the gap, designing game mechanics on the fly, building rapid paper prototypes, and refactoring physics constraints to ensure stable 60fps performance on early iPad hardware.

## 04 // Breakthroughs, IP & Cultural Legacy
Air Quest demonstrated how serious environmental telemetry could be transformed into playful civic software. It was deployed across Central Valley educational pilots, providing a blueprint for game-based pediatric health interventions.
